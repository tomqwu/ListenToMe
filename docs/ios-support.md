# Listen To Me — iPhone and iPad support

Contact **[tom@cloudnativity.io](mailto:tom@cloudnativity.io)** for help. Include your iOS version, device model, and the app version shown in Settings → About. Do not include API keys or private transcripts.

## Getting started

Requires iOS or iPadOS 26 or later. Tap Start listening and allow microphone access. Speech language assets may download on first use. More → Settings → Transcription → Language lists every language this device can transcribe, grouped as Installed and Downloadable (a downloadable one fetches its speech model from Apple the first time you record in it); your language choice is remembered across launches. With the language set to Automatic (the default), the app detects the language you speak on your device during the first seconds of each recording and, if it differs, switches and transcribes the recording again. It checks again about once a minute while people talk, so a recording that changes language part-way switches too, and only the part since the change is transcribed again ("Detected … from 2:05 · switched"); the first time, a language model of about 75 MB downloads from huggingface.co. Detection runs on this device, and no audio is sent anywhere for it. If you had chosen a language before version 1.12.0, it stays fixed; choose Automatic to turn detection on. Tap Undo next to "Detected … · switched" to go back to the language before the latest switch and keep it (earlier switches stay; Undo is briefly unavailable while the app is switching), or choose a fixed language in More → Settings → Transcription. A recording that continues the conversation, for example after a call, stays in the detected language. "Switched for new speech" means the audio before the switch was no longer held (the app keeps only the last two minutes), so the earlier lines were not transcribed again. Recording works while the app is open; it does not record other apps or phone calls. A call, Siri or an alarm pauses capture, the status line says why, and recording resumes when the interruption ends.

Use Notes to add context, Save to keep a conversation, and History to reopen, search or share it. History search finds words and, in languages with an on-device model (such as English), related meaning too: a conversation found by meaning is marked Related, and each result shows the matching excerpt. To ask a question across your conversations, type it in the search field and tap "Ask your history"; the answer lists its sources, and tapping one opens that conversation. With Apple Intelligence the answer is made on this device; with another provider, the line under the button names where the matching excerpts are sent. To remove everything at once, use Delete all conversations… at the bottom of History or in More → Settings → Privacy. It asks first, cannot be undone and is unavailable while recording. Recordings still arriving from Apple Watch and notes already written by Save to folder are kept. A conversation still called “New conversation” names itself after you stop or save, once it has about 30 words. The title comes from its summary, from Apple Intelligence or your own Ollama server, or from keywords found on this device; with a cloud service, nothing is sent for a title. Type a title and it is yours: it is never replaced. To keep the default names, turn off More → Settings → Conversations → Name conversations automatically. Sharing offers readable text and Markdown; Export as saves a file (see below). More → What's New shows the release version and recent changes, and appears after an update only when those notes actually changed. More → Settings → About → Acknowledgements lists the open-source packages in the app, each with its license.

## Save audio recordings

More → Settings → Audio recordings → Save audio recordings is off by default, and the app keeps no audio until you turn it on. When it is on, each recording also saves its microphone audio with the conversation on this device (about 12 MB per hour), always left out of iCloud and Finder backups. While recording, the status line says "Saving audio on this device". To listen, open the conversation, tap the play button above the live transcript (or expand the transcript), and tap a line's time to play from it; Share audio exports the `.m4a` file. If storage runs low, the app stops saving audio, says so in orange under the title, and keeps transcribing. Deleting a conversation deletes its audio; Settings offers Delete all saved audio and Keep audio (until you delete it, or 7, 30 or 90 days). Apple Watch recordings keep no audio. If the app quits unexpectedly while recording, that part keeps all but its last few seconds. Turning the setting off during a recording stops saving at once and keeps what was saved; Delete all saved audio waits until you stop recording.

## Save to folder (Obsidian and Markdown)

More → Settings → Save to folder is off by default. Turn it on and pick a folder in Files — On My iPhone, iCloud Drive, or your Obsidian vault folder. After you stop listening or tap Save, the conversation is written there as one Markdown note named like `2026-10-23 1430 ListenToMe 1a2b3c4d.md`, with front matter (date, duration, language, speakers, the `listentome` tag), then the summaries, notes and a transcript with speaker names and timestamps. Nothing is written line by line while you record. When a summary, the title, notes or speaker names change later, the same note is updated instead of a new one appearing; other files in the folder are never touched, and a file is replaced only if its front matter names this conversation. If you rename a note in Obsidian, the app leaves it alone and the next update writes a new note under the usual name. Save all conversations now writes the conversations already in History. Deleting a conversation in History does not delete its note. The note's language is the one the conversation was actually transcribed in — the fixed language, or the one Automatic switched to (a conversation that changed language part-way lists both) — so changing the language setting later never rewrites it. Conversations last saved by an earlier version, until you record in them again, have no language line.

## Export as a file (subtitles, plain text, JSON)

More → Export as, or touch and hold a conversation in History → Export as, saves the conversation as a file: Markdown, plain text, Subtitles (SRT), Subtitles (WebVTT) or JSON. The share sheet opens with the file, so Save to Files, Mail or AirDrop get a real `.srt` or `.json`. Files are named after the conversation's title and date, like `Weekly sync 2026-10-23 1435.srt`. Transcript lines carry `[mm:ss]` timestamps from the recording, including Apple Watch recordings; subtitles show the speaker's name when more than one person speaks and split long lines into readable captions. A conversation with only imported notes has no timestamps, so subtitles are not offered for it. The JSON format is documented, with every field, in [Export formats](export-formats.md).

## Vocabularies and My terms

More → Settings → Vocabularies has seven presets — Medical, Legal, Software & Cloud, Engineering, Finance, Sales and Education & Research — and My terms, your own list of names, products and jargon in any language. Turn a preset on with Use by default, or tap Add when the app suggests one ("Sounds like a medical conversation — add the Medical vocabulary?" appears after about a minute of speech; the check runs on this device, and nothing turns on unless you tap Add). Not now hides it for this conversation; Don't suggest … again stops it for that preset (Settings has Suggest … again), and Suggest vocabularies turns suggestions off. Add a word from a transcript line by long-pressing the line → Add to My terms…; import or export My terms as a plain-text file with one term per line. Active terms help speech recognition (up to 100: My terms first), AI speech correction and summaries; Summary is organized by the domain's headings unless you turn off Organize summaries by domain. Presets are English-first. The Medical vocabulary does not make the app a medical device: do not use transcripts or AI output for clinical decisions. If a word is still misheard, add it to My terms in the spelling you want.

## Speaker labels

Identify speakers is on unless you turn it off in More → Settings → Speakers (the switch is available while not recording). Identification runs on this device and uses more battery while listening. The speaker models download once from huggingface.co: when the app opens with the setting on, when you turn it on, or when you start a recording, and recording never waits for them. Settings → Speakers shows the download and says when the models are ready, or why they failed, with Retry. With the models ready, the status under the conversation title reads "Speaker labels on". While the models download, or when labels are unavailable, a speaker line appears below it ("preparing models…", or "unavailable" with Retry). Record as usual: lines start as Microphone and change to Speaker 1, Speaker 2… after the first pass, about 20 seconds into a recording, and the last lines are labeled when you stop, so a short recording is labeled at Stop once the models are ready. To rename a speaker, open Notes → Speakers, type the name and press Done; the new name appears on every line, in History search and in shared text and Markdown. Summaries pick up the new name the next time they are generated. Labels are estimates: overlapping speech and very short phrases often keep Microphone. Within one recording, someone who stays quiet for a while keeps their label and name when they speak again, though two very similar voices can occasionally be mixed up or split. A voice is not recognized again after you stop and start listening. Status details (under Quick Summary) lists whether Identify speakers is on, whether the models are ready, how many passes ran and the last speaker error.

## AI summaries

A new install summarizes on-device with Apple Intelligence where the device supports it. To use Ollama instead, enter your own Ollama Cloud API key in Settings, or the address of an Ollama server you run on your own network — use the computer's `.local` name, for example `http://your-mac.local:11434`, because a plain-`http` numeric address such as `192.168.1.10` is refused. Ollama account limits apply and internet access is required for cloud features. The app is free; third-party service access may have its own costs.

To use an OpenAI-compatible server — LM Studio, vLLM, llama.cpp, Ollama's `/v1` API or a hosted service such as OpenRouter — choose **OpenAI-compatible** as the summary provider, enter the server's base URL (for example `http://your-mac.local:1234/v1` or `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1`) and tap **Save server URL**. Add an API key only if the server needs one. Tap **Refresh models from server**, choose a model for each role, then **Test connection**. Settings says "your server" for an address on this device or your network and "may leave this device" for anything else. Auto and Quick need the model to answer in a specific JSON format: open the Quick model and tap **Test this model** to check. If it fails, Auto pauses and says so; choose another Quick model, or keep using Summary, which does not need that format.

To use a cloud AI service — OpenAI, Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral or xAI Grok — open **Summary provider**, choose the service under **Cloud AI**, tap **Get a key from…** to create an API key on the vendor's site, paste it and tap **Save API key**. Then **Refresh models from…**, choose a model for each role (suggested models such as Claude Opus 5.5 are marked, but nothing is chosen for you) and **Test connection**; open the Quick model and tap **Test this model** to check that it can drive Auto. Each service says "Sends your transcript to" that vendor and keeps its own key; switching services keeps every key. You pay the vendor for what you use.

Auto reacts to new speech. Quick provides short key points; Summary updates when the conversation merits a fuller review. Both can also be generated manually. The iPhone and iPad app has two answers, Quick Summary (on the Live tab) and Summary (on the Summary tab); Deep is on the Mac app only. A conversation saved with a Deep analysis by an earlier version still has it: open it from History and expand Earlier deep analysis under the Summary. It is read-only and is kept in shared text, exports and Save to folder notes. When Auto appears inactive, open Status details and check the provider and model status. A brief pause to combine incoming speech is normal; unchanged input does not require a new summary.

Under Quick Summary, **Suggested · from context** may offer two or three short questions about the conversation, such as "Who owns the Thursday review?". They appear after an Auto check or after you refresh Quick Summary, and making them costs no extra request. Tap one to ask it: a small card under Quick Summary shows your question in bold with the answer below it, and your summaries stay as they were. The answer is not saved with the conversation, so copy it if you want to keep it, and Close removes it. Nothing is sent unless you tap. If a question cannot be sent right now — for example while another answer is being written — the app says why and the suggestion stays. Suggestions change at most every 20 seconds, a question that was answered is not suggested again, and they clear when you start a new conversation or change the AI provider or model. With Auto off and no refresh, there are none.

## Summarize part of a conversation

To summarize just some lines, tap the checklist button on the Live transcript (or Select in the expanded transcript), tap the lines you want or drag down their circles, then tap Summarize selection. You can also touch and hold a line and choose Summarize from here (that line to the latest) or Select from here. Lines still being transcribed can't be selected. The summary appears in a sheet with Copy, Share and Add to notes, and the last five are kept with the conversation under its Summary and included when you share, export or save it to a folder. Only the selected lines are sent, with their times and speaker names (plus your active vocabulary and My terms, as for other summaries), to the provider you chose for summaries: Apple Intelligence keeps them on the device, and with any other provider the sheet names it first ("Sends the selected lines to …") and sends only when you tap Summarize. Cancel stops a summary that is still being written; the sheet says Cancelled and you can summarize again. If Summarize selection (or Summarize from here in the touch-and-hold menu) is greyed out, the line under it says why, for example a missing key or another answer still being written. While a selection summary is being written, Summary and Quick Summary wait for it, and the other way round. Text in the transcript stays selectable for copying, except in Select mode, where a tap picks the whole line. A very long selection is shortened to its first and latest lines, and the sheet says so.

## Common fixes

- No transcript: check microphone permission, selected language and downloaded speech assets.
- Transcript in the wrong language: Automatic checks the first stretch of speech, then again about once a minute while people talk. A later change of language must be heard twice in a row (about 20 seconds apart) before it switches, so a short phrase in another language does not switch, and a switch stays for at least 90 seconds; a recording switches at most four times. A change is noticed one to two minutes after it happens. If a speech model has to download first, the part transcribed again starts at the oldest audio still held, and the notice says from when. Re-checks pause in Low Power Mode, while the app is in the background, while the device is hot and during silence. Detection needs about 8 seconds of speech and is skipped when it is not confident, and it cannot switch to a language your device has no speech model for. Words from two languages mixed within one sentence are not supported: the transcriber hears one language at a time. Choose the language in More → Settings → Transcription to fix it for every recording.
- "Language detection paused": the language model could not be downloaded or loaded, for example without internet on first use. Recording continues in the starting language; the next recording tries again.
- Cloud error: test the connection in Settings, check your key and account limits, and refresh the available model list. For your own server, check the `.local` address and allow local network access.
- OpenAI-compatible server errors: "Plain http:// is only allowed…" means the address needs `https://` or the computer's `.local` name. "The server answered with a redirect" means you should enter the final address shown by your server or provider. HTTP 401/403 means the key was rejected; HTTP 404 usually means the URL does not end in the API base (such as `/v1`) or the model name is wrong. "did not follow the Quick Summary format" means that model cannot drive Auto; pick another Quick model. A model with a small context window (LM Studio's default is a few thousand tokens) may reject a long conversation; increase its context length on the server.
- Cloud AI service errors: "rejected the API key" (HTTP 401/403) means the key is wrong, revoked or for another service — paste it again from the vendor's key page. "overloaded" or "rate limiting" (HTTP 429/529) means the vendor is busy or your plan's limit is reached; Claude requests retry once when Anthropic says how long to wait; otherwise try later. "Claude declined to answer this request" is a refusal from Claude; rephrase or choose another model. "cut off because it reached the length limit" keeps the partial text; ask for a shorter answer. A Gemini key error appears as HTTP 400 "Please pass a valid API key".
- "Using the last ~5 min of the conversation." (or "the opening … and the last …") above a Quick answer: the conversation was too long for the model, so the answer read only that part — plus your Summary, when you have one. A Summary generated earlier gives the best grounding; a model with a larger window, such as a cloud AI service, reads more. "The answer was cut off at the model's output limit.": the model stopped before finishing; the partial text is kept. Generate again, or raise the model's output limit on your server.
- Apple Intelligence unavailable: check device support and enable Apple Intelligence in system settings, or select Ollama, a cloud AI service or an OpenAI-compatible server.
- Recording stopped: backgrounding stops recording and saves the conversation; a call, Siri or an alarm pauses it and it resumes on its own.
- "Save to folder" shows an orange line under the conversation title: the note could not be written, and the line says why. If the folder was moved, renamed, deleted or access was lost, choose it again in Settings → Save to folder (Change folder…). If storage is full, free up space and tap Retry. If the note is still downloading from iCloud, wait for it to download and tap Retry. If a different note already has the name, it is left as it is and the line says so. One note that can't be written doesn't stop the others. Unwritten notes are remembered across launches and are tried again on the next save or with Retry. Notes waiting for a folder show the same line until you choose one.
- No speaker labels yet: read the status under the conversation title. "Preparing models" means the one-time download is still running; a recording that ends before it finishes keeps Microphone, because Stop never starts or waits for the download. Once it says "Speaker labels on", even a short recording is labeled when you stop.
- "Speaker labels unavailable": the speaker models could not be loaded, for example without internet on first use. Check your connection and tap Retry next to the line or in Settings → Speakers; starting a new recording also tries again. Recording and transcription keep working meanwhile.
- Missing from a restore: check whether "Exclude conversations from iCloud backup" is on in Settings.

Read the [privacy policy](ios-privacy.md) for storage, optional cloud processing and deletion information.

## Apple Watch language detection (not yet distributed)

When the Watch companion becomes available, Automatic will check the first stretch of speech using
an already-downloaded language model on your iPhone. It never downloads that model while importing.
If the model is unavailable, speech is too short or detection is uncertain, it uses the device language.
The recording keeps that choice through retries and later transfers. Choose a fixed transcription
language before importing if you need to override Automatic. Audio stays on the iPhone for detection.

In landscape Duo, the pane-width button beside Live/Summary gives more room to the transcript or
Quick Summary, or restores equal widths. The choice is remembered. Use Transcript actions (the
ellipsis menu) for line selection, speech correction settings, and saved recording playback.
