# Competition Analysis

_Last updated: 2026-10 (ListenToMe's own features; competitor facts were last checked 2026-09). All pricing and feature facts are stated as of 2026; where a detail could not be confirmed from a primary source, it is qualified with "approximately" or "reportedly."_

## The category: AI meeting assistants and real-time copilots

Over the last few years, "AI meeting assistant" has grown from a niche transcription utility into a crowded product category. The tools in this space cluster into a few overlapping shapes:

- **Bot-joining note-takers** that send a visible recording bot into Zoom / Google Meet / Microsoft Teams calls, then produce transcripts, summaries, and action items afterward (Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai, Fathom, Fellow, tl;dv).
- **Bot-free desktop capturers** that listen to your computer's own microphone and system audio locally and augment your notes without joining the call as a participant (Granola, Superpowered).
- **Real-time copilots / interview-assist overlays** that surface live answer suggestions during a call, sometimes explicitly marketed as "undetectable" (Cluely and a wider category of interview-assist tools).
- **On-device transcription tools** that run speech-to-text fully on the local machine for privacy-sensitive users (MacWhisper, and the open-source Natively, Hyprnote/Anarlog, and Meetily).

Two structural tensions define the category. First, **privacy vs. convenience**: nearly every commercial product processes audio and runs its AI in the cloud, even when it markets itself as "local-first" — the local part is usually just audio _capture_, while transcription and summarization happen on third-party servers. Second, **opinionated vs. open**: most products lock you into a single (often undisclosed) transcription engine and a single summarization LLM with no user choice, whereas a small set of privacy-first / open tools let you bring your own model.

**ListenToMe** sits deliberately at the intersection of the privacy-first and copilot shapes: a fully on-device macOS meeting copilot that transcribes live with Apple's SpeechAnalyzer and gives real-time AI help through Ollama (local and cloud models) or Apple Intelligence, with per-pane model selection, language selection, use-case presets, file/folder context, local calendar context, opt-in on-device speaker diarization, keyword History search, and Markdown/recap/PDF export (macOS 1.4.4, the latest release) — free and open-source. A companion iPhone/iPad app for in-person recording is in TestFlight (1.17.0 (34)) and already ships on-device semantic search with "Ask your history", Markdown/plain-text/SRT/WebVTT/JSON export, OpenAI-compatible servers and opt-in cloud AI presets; the same features are merged for macOS but not yet released.

## Per-competitor detail

### Granola
Granola is a bot-free "AI notepad for back-to-back meetings" running natively on macOS, Windows, and iOS that captures your computer's own system audio and mic rather than joining calls as a visible bot, so it works across Zoom, Meet, Teams, and Webex. Despite "local-first" marketing, audio is streamed to third-party cloud ASR (it names Deepgram and AssemblyAI) and summarization runs on OpenAI/Anthropic models in the cloud; raw audio is discarded after transcription and text is stored encrypted in US AWS, with de-identified self-training offered on an opt-out basis. Pricing as of 2026 is approximately a free Basic tier, Business ~$14/user/mo, and Enterprise ~$35/user/mo. Source: <https://granola.ai/security>

### Otter.ai
Otter is a cloud-based assistant for web, iOS, Android, desktop, and Chrome whose "OtterPilot" bot auto-joins Zoom/Teams/Meet for real-time transcription, summaries, action items, and Otter AI Chat, and in 2026 it repositioned as a "Conversational Knowledge Engine" with an MCP server. It uses its own proprietary ASR (explicitly not Whisper) and leans on Anthropic's Claude for summarization; notably, its privacy policy permits training proprietary models on de-identified audio and transcriptions that "may contain Personal Information," which sits in tension with its no-training marketing. Pricing as of 2026 is approximately a free tier (300 min/mo), Pro ~$8.33–16.99/user/mo, and Business ~$20–30/user/mo. Source: <https://otter.ai/privacy-security>

### Fireflies.ai
Fireflies is a cloud-based assistant (web, macOS/Windows desktop, mobile, Chrome extension) whose bot records and transcribes Zoom/Teams/Meet/Webex, processing and storing data in its US cloud (AWS + GCP) with a zero-day vendor-retention policy and a stated no-training-on-customer-content position. It is reportedly Whisper-based for transcription (third-party attribution, not officially confirmed) with its own AI layer for summaries, the AskFred chat assistant, and the Live Assist real-time copilot added in late 2025. Pricing as of 2026 is approximately free, Pro ~$10–18/seat/mo, Business ~$19–29, and Enterprise ~$39. Source: <https://fireflies.ai/security>

### Fathom
Fathom is a cloud-based AI meeting assistant for Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams with a desktop app and a 2026 iOS app, storing data in US/Canada AWS and holding SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, and GDPR with AI sub-processors contractually barred from training on customer data. A 2026 update added bot-free capture, account-wide insights, and live summaries; "bring your own LLM" is delivered via its public API/MCP integrations rather than an in-app model picker. Pricing as of 2026 runs from a free tier through Business at $34/user/mo, with a general-meeting focus that leans toward sales/conversation intelligence. Source: <https://www.fathom.ai/pricing>

### Fellow
Fellow began as a meeting-agenda/productivity tool and is now an AI-first meeting assistant (Fellow.ai), notable for being the most cross-platform of the bot-joiners — macOS/Windows desktop, iOS/Android, and Chrome/Firefox extensions, with both bot and botless capture across Meet/Zoom/Teams/Slack. It has a strong privacy posture (SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA; AWS Canada; a "Zero-Day Retention" option) and, per third-party reporting, routes its AI layer through AssemblyAI's LLM Gateway with no user-facing model picker. Pricing as of 2026 runs approximately Free → Team ~$7 → Business ~$15 → Enterprise ~$25/user/mo. Source: <https://help.fellow.ai/en/articles/4302231-security-and-compliance>

### tl;dv
tl;dv is a cloud-based assistant for Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams positioned primarily around sales/revenue teams (CRM sync, sales coaching, MEDDIC/BANT/SPIN scoring), with EU-based data storage and SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR, and EU AI Act compliance. It uses OpenAI Whisper (via Google Cloud) for transcription and chunks/anonymizes meetings before sending them to Anthropic for summaries, with no user-selectable LLMs. Pricing as of 2026 runs from a generous free tier through Pro ~$18/user/mo and Business ~$59/user/mo (annual) to custom Enterprise. Source: <https://tldv.io/features/security-commitment/>

### Cluely
Cluely is a real-time, screen-overlay AI assistant for live calls (interviews, sales, exams) that listens, transcribes, and surfaces hotkey-triggered answer suggestions, with a premium "Undetectability" mode that renders the overlay via low-level GPU hooks so it stays hidden from screen sharing. As of 2026 it offers a macOS desktop app and an iOS app; it is cloud-based, does not disclose which LLMs it uses, and does not allow model choice. It is explicitly not privacy-first and reportedly suffered a 2025 data breach exposing 80,000+ users; pricing is approximately a free Starter, Pro $19.99/mo, and Pro + Undetectability $149.99/mo. Source: <https://cluely.com/pricing>

### Interview-assist tools (category)
A crowded 2026 category of real-time AI overlays designed to feed live answers during interviews while evading screen-capture detection, including Final Round AI (~$149/mo, supports coding rounds), Interview Coder (coding-interview focused), LockedIn AI (~$54.99–119.99/mo plus a stealth mode), and others such as Interview Sidekick, Parakeet AI, and Verve AI. The common technique is system-level/GPU rendering that bypasses the screen-capture layer; transcription engines and LLMs are generally undisclosed and model choice is rarely offered. These tools share ListenToMe's "real-time copilot" shape but are narrowly aimed at interviews and are cloud-based. Source: <https://www.finalroundai.com/blog/best-undetectable-ai-interview-tools>

### Natively (open-source)
Natively is an open-source, free, local-first alternative to Cluely/Otter/Granola, doing on-device speech-to-text (ONNX Moonshine + Whisper-large-v3-turbo, accelerated via CoreML/Metal or DirectML) and storing transcripts, embeddings, and keys locally by default while letting users plug in cloud LLMs. It runs on macOS 12+ and Windows 10/11 (with a community Linux build), offers a real-time copilot, summaries, action items, local RAG/semantic search, diarization, and personas, and has a strong BYOK/multi-model story (Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, Groq, and local Ollama). Pricing as of 2026 is free for personal/non-commercial use with a Pro lifetime/yearly tier; it is the closest direct philosophical competitor to ListenToMe, though more interview-focused and cross-platform rather than macOS-native. Source: <https://github.com/Natively-AI-assistant/natively-cluely-ai-assistant>

### Hyprnote (now Anarlog, open-source)
Hyprnote is the project formerly known by that name at `github.com/fastrepl/hyprnote`; as of 2026 the README there describes the open-source meeting-notetaker component as "Anarlog," billed as "the privacy-first AI meeting notepad" that is "Open source, local-first, and yours to fork." It is a Tauri v2 desktop app for macOS, Windows, and Linux (mobile source exists but no app is currently distributed) that captures audio directly without a bot joining the call. Transcription can run on-device (Apple Speech or its own Soniqo engine) or, if selected, through cloud providers (Deepgram Nova, Soniox, AssemblyAI); summarization/chat similarly can run against a local model or an OpenAI-compatible server (Ollama, LM Studio, Unsloth) or a "Managed Auto" cloud option that reportedly routes through Claude Sonnet via OpenRouter. Sessions, notes, and transcripts are stored in local SQLite with plain local recording/attachment files and Markdown export. The community app is MIT-licensed and free; enterprise components are commercially licensed, with cloud AI and sync described as opt-in rather than required. Source: <https://github.com/fastrepl/hyprnote>

### Meetily (open-source)
Meetily, from Zackriya Solutions, is a self-hosted, open-source "privacy-first AI meeting assistant that captures, transcribes, and summarizes meetings entirely on your infrastructure," for macOS, Windows, and Linux (`.dmg`/`.exe` installers, Linux via source build). Its core pipeline runs locally: "All processing happens locally on your device" and "No data ever leaves your computer," with local Whisper (multiple sizes) or Parakeet (ONNX) transcription, GPU-accelerated via Metal/CoreML on macOS or Vulkan on Windows, plus professional dual-source audio mixing. For summarization it recommends local Ollama but also supports cloud LLMs (Claude, Groq, OpenRouter, OpenAI) and custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints when a cloud model is explicitly chosen. The Community Edition is free and MIT-licensed; a paid Meetily PRO tier and an Enterprise tier (100+ seats, managed compliance) are also offered. Source: <https://github.com/Zackriya-Solutions/meeting-minutes>

### Superpowered
Superpowered is a bot-free AI meeting notetaker for macOS and Windows that captures device audio live and generates structured AI notes via templates, emphasizing a no-recordings privacy posture: audio is deleted immediately after live transcription and transcripts are kept only ~7 days, with SOC 2 Type 2 and GDPR compliance. Its transcription engine is not officially specified (third-party reports cite Deepgram), and although AI processing is cloud-based, it advertises no multi-model or BYO-model support. Pricing as of 2026 is approximately a free tier (10 AI notes/mo), Basic $25/mo, and Pro $50/mo. Source: <https://superpowered.me/>

### MacWhisper
MacWhisper is a polished Mac transcription app built on OpenAI Whisper that runs inference fully on-device — audio never leaves the machine by default — making it popular with journalists, lawyers, and therapists. It primarily transcribes audio/video files, URLs, and meetings (with real-time/system-audio capture as a secondary feature) and offers optional AI post-processing (cleanup, summaries, action items, chat) via your own API keys (OpenAI, Anthropic/Claude, Google, Groq in the Gumroad version) or a paid cloud Assistant subscription. Pricing as of 2026 is approximately a free tier, a Gumroad Pro at ~€59/$69 one-time, and an App Store version at $6.99/mo to $99.99 lifetime. Source: <https://goodsnooze.gumroad.com/l/macwhisper>

## Positioning for ListenToMe

ListenToMe occupies a corner of the market that almost no commercial competitor genuinely fills: a **fully on-device, real-time meeting copilot** that is also **free and open-source**. The differentiators stack up against the field as follows:

- **Fully on-device and private.** Transcription runs locally via Apple SpeechAnalyzer, and AI help can run entirely against a local Ollama model — so audio and transcript need never leave the Mac. Granola, Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, Fellow, tl;dv, Cluely, and Superpowered all process audio and/or run their AI in the cloud, even when marketed as "local-first." MacWhisper and the open-source Natively, Hyprnote/Anarlog, and Meetily are now the closest peers on this axis — "on-device + BYO + free/OSS" is table stakes within that open-source cluster rather than a differentiator — but Hyprnote/Anarlog's default STT and AI paths are configurable to cloud providers rather than local-only, and none of these peers pair local transcription with ListenToMe's live, hotkey-driven copilot loop (⌘⇧Space proactive Quick answers via a question detector) or Apple Intelligence as a no-Ollama on-device model path.
- **Bring-your-own-model (Ollama local + cloud).** ListenToMe lets users choose their own models — local via Ollama or cloud — rather than being locked to a single undisclosed backend LLM. Most commercial competitors (Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, Fellow, tl;dv, Cluely, Superpowered) offer no user-facing model choice at all; MacWhisper, Natively, Hyprnote/Anarlog, and Meetily are all comparable on BYO-model support now.
- **Multi-pane, multi-model.** Per-pane model selection lets a user run different models side by side for the same conversation (for example a fast local model for live notes and a stronger model for analysis). No mainstream competitor exposes this multi-pane / multi-model workflow.
- **Multi-purpose via use-case presets.** Rather than being pinned to a single vertical (sales for tl;dv, interviews for Cluely, file transcription for MacWhisper), use-case presets plus file/folder context let one app serve meetings, interviews, study, support, and more.
- **Free and open-source.** No subscription, no seat pricing, and the code is open for inspection — a sharp contrast to the $14–149/mo commercial tiers and the closed, undisclosed pipelines of most of the category.

**One-line positioning:** _ListenToMe is the free, open-source, fully on-device meeting copilot for macOS — bring your own model, run it private, and shape it to any conversation._

## Gaps / opportunities

Most of the gaps this analysis originally identified have since closed, all on-device. Each is
marked with where it is available: a macOS release, an iPhone/iPad TestFlight build, or **macOS
unreleased** (merged to `main` after macOS 1.4.4 and waiting for the next macOS release). Details are
in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

- **Calendar context** — shipped: macOS 1.1.0, iPhone 1.4.0 (11). EventKit reads the current/next event from the local Calendar and pre-loads it as meeting context — parity with the auto-context Granola, Fathom, Fellow, and Superpowered offer. Auto-*starting* capture on a detected meeting is still open ([#54](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/54)).
- **Speaker diarization** — shipped: macOS 1.2.0 (experimental, opt-in), iPhone 1.11.0 (28), on by default since iPhone 1.12.0 (29). macOS runs FluidAudio (CoreML Pyannote) over the Others channel locally, labels transcript lines "Speaker N", and shows per-voice talk-time — the axis Otter, Fireflies, and Natively led on. The iPhone/iPad app labels and renames the people its one microphone hears (#180). A returning voice keeps its label (iPhone 1.13.0 (30); macOS unreleased, #171). Still open: labels for Apple Watch recordings ([#192](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/192)).
- **On-device peer set has grown.** Hyprnote/Anarlog and Meetily joined Natively and MacWhisper as free/open-source, on-device-capable meeting tools since this analysis was first written — none of them offer ListenToMe's per-pane multi-model routing, live hotkey copilot loop, or Apple Intelligence on-device path with zero model download.
- **Richer exports** — Markdown (macOS 1.0.0), a concise shareable recap and PDF (macOS 1.1.0), and copy-to-clipboard are released on macOS. Plain text, SRT and WebVTT subtitles and versioned JSON, named after the conversation, shipped in iPhone 1.15.0 (32) and are macOS unreleased ([#130](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/130)), matching MacWhisper's subtitle exports. Timestamps appear where the engine reports them (WhisperKit and iPhone recordings); macOS SpeechAnalyzer timestamps are [#68](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/68), and marking each line's language after an Automatic switch is [#227](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/227).
- **Automatic meeting titles** — shipped: iPhone 1.15.0 (32); macOS unreleased (#221). Otter, Granola and Fireflies name meetings for you. A saved conversation that still has its default name gets a short title from its summary, from the user's own summary model, or from on-device keywords, and a typed or calendar title is never replaced.
- **Cross-meeting search and "ask your history".** Keyword History search shipped in macOS 1.1.0. Hybrid keyword + semantic search, with the semantic index built on the device from Apple NaturalLanguage embeddings, and a question answered from the top matching excerpts with cited source conversations — the Ask Granola / AskFred / Natively-RAG use case, without a cloud index — shipped in iPhone 1.15.0 (32) and is macOS unreleased ([#57](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/57)). Only the excerpts reach the user's chosen model, and they stay on the device with local Ollama or Apple Intelligence. Apple's on-device embeddings are weaker than the large embedding models cloud RAG uses, and languages without an on-device model fall back to keyword search; more languages and cheaper re-indexing are [#223](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/223).
- **Domain vocabulary** — Simplified Chinese terms and on-device suggestions shipped on iPhone 1.16.0 (33), with macOS unreleased (#238); other languages retain the English lists. Previously shipped: iPhone 1.15.0 (32); macOS unreleased (#222). Seven curated domain vocabularies (Medical, Legal, Software & Cloud,
  Engineering, Finance, Sales, Education & Research) plus a user "My terms" list bias on-device
  recognition, guide AI correction and summaries, and lay summaries out by domain; an on-device
  classifier offers the right one mid-conversation. Commercial tools such as Otter and
  Fireflies advertise custom-vocabulary lists on paid tiers (reported; feature names and limits vary),
  but they apply them in their cloud ASR; here the list, the suggestion and recognition stay on the
  device. Per-conversation choices are saved and restored, and Auto Quick receives their bounded glossary
  (unreleased, #237). Stronger recognition biasing remains [#236](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/236).
- **Automatic language** — shipped: iPhone 1.12.0 (29) detects the spoken language on the device and is the default, and iPhone 1.13.0 (30) re-checks it mid-recording (#197, #199). On macOS the opt-in WhisperKit engine detects and mixes languages. Mixed-language sentences on iPhone are [#205](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/205).
- **Saved audio** — shipped: iPhone 1.15.0 (32); macOS unreleased (#55). Opt-in, off by default, kept only on the device and out of backups, with tap-a-line playback. Re-transcribing with an on-device preview and explicit replacement is implemented for the next train ([#224](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/224)).
- **Summarize a selection** — shipped: iPhone 1.15.0 (32) (#234): pick transcript lines and summarize only those. Not on macOS yet, where transcript lines cannot be selected ([#126](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/126)).
- **Save to folder (Obsidian-friendly notes)** — shipped: iPhone 1.13.0 (30); macOS unreleased (#53). One Markdown note per conversation, with front matter, written to a folder the user chooses and updated in place.
- **Presets library** — shipped on macOS: 12 use-case presets in 1.0.0 and six more in 1.1.0, 18 in all (1:1, standup, sales call, interviews, lecture, support, retro, negotiation, and more) against the dozens of templates the commercial tools ship. The iPhone app has no presets.

**Bring your own model, wider.** OpenAI-compatible endpoints (LM Studio, vLLM, llama.cpp, OpenRouter and any `/v1/chat/completions` server) shipped in iPhone 1.13.0 (30) and are macOS unreleased ([#52](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/52)), closing the gap with Hyprnote/Anarlog and Meetily, with honest "your server" / "may leave this device" labelling, a key bound to its server and no redirect following. One-tap cloud AI presets — OpenAI, Anthropic Claude (over Anthropic's native Messages API), Google Gemini, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral and xAI Grok — followed in iPhone 1.14.0 (31) and are macOS unreleased ([#214](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/214)), matching Natively's bring-your-own-key vendor list without typing URLs. They stay opt-in with nothing selected by default, each is labelled "Sends your transcript to <vendor>", each vendor keeps its own Keychain key, and no vendor SDK is linked. Context-aware **Suggested** prompt chips ([#61](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/61); iPhone 1.15.0 (32), macOS unreleased) — two or three one-tap requests drawn from the live conversation, sent only on a tap — ride on the Auto Quick check (and on iOS a manual Quick refresh, Apple Intelligence included) rather than a model call of their own.

**What remains** is a set of **future ideas, not commitments**, each tracked as an [open GitHub issue](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues) rather than duplicated here: meeting auto-detection / auto-start ([#54](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/54)), a structured open/to-do/decided grid for action items ([#62](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/62)), more WhisperKit model sizes ([#58](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/58)), and on macOS a free-form Ask input ([#103](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/103)) and reopening a saved conversation ([#104](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/104)). Direct Notion sync was closed as deferred: it would need its own export and privacy design ([#59](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/59)). The [iOS app](IOS.md) is a personal capture and recall companion in TestFlight, not a port of the macOS copilot, and an Apple Watch companion that records for it is built but not shipped ([#181](https://github.com/tomqwu/ListenToMe/issues/181)). Android and Mac↔iOS companion/sync remain out of scope.
